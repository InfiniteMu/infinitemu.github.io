\researchsubsection{Job Market Paper}
\vspace{0.35em}

\textbf{\href{https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/syjdmc11tqs9vkmqjc36p/punishment.pdf?rlkey=96ybr1n3bgd9m4yh0dgkh5x24&dl=0}{Competition and Punishment in Expert Markets}}\par
\vspace{0.22em}
{\small This paper studies how punishment affects expert misconduct when detecting fraud requires conflicting recommendations from competing providers. Stronger penalties can sustain honest advice without eliminating fraudulent equilibria and can increase fraud and prices. Expected punishment remains bounded even as nominal penalties grow arbitrarily large.\par}

\vspace{0.85em}
\researchsubsection{Working Papers}
\vspace{0.35em}

\textbf{\href{https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7423579}{Information Differentiation under Correlation Neglect}}\par
With Xinyang Wang. Submitted.\par
\vspace{0.22em}
{\small Correlation neglect can induce providers to differentiate. Consumers who neglect correlation when updating but correctly assess learning efficiency prefer both sources only below a correlation cutoff. To retain these consumers, the less accurate provider must sufficiently differentiate its information, creating an equilibrium cap on correlation that improves learning whenever it lowers correlation.\par}
